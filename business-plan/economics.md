# Economics & Insurance Reference

## Pricing

- Individual sessions: **$150/hr**
- Group sessions (2+ students): **$85/hr per student**
- Home-visit premium vs. office rate: consider a flat travel fee (~$30–50/session)
  instead of a fully separate hourly rate — easier to justify and scales better
  across towns with different drive times

## Cost Assumptions (validate with real quotes)

- Tutor pay: **~$40/hr** (above the $20–35/hr Boston-area market average, to attract
  Harvard/MIT/BU/Northeastern-credentialed tutors)
- Cambridge space: ~$3,000–3,200/month for a multi-room suite (not the $400
  single-office headline rate — that's for one room, not four concurrent tutoring
  rooms)
- Newton/Lexington space: ~$2,400–2,800/month, based on $28–34/sqft commercial rates
  (Lexington unconfirmed — get a real quote)

## Steady-State Model (3 locations, fully staffed, full daily utilization)

- Per location per day: 16 group-student-hours × $85 + 4 individual-student-hours ×
  $150, at 1.5 hrs/session ≈ **$2,940/day**
- Per location annualized (6 days/week): **~$917,000/year**
- 3 locations combined: **~$2.75M/year revenue**, **~$1.7M/year net contribution**
  after tutor payroll, rent, and insurance/admin — before your own role, curriculum
  R&D, or marketing spend

**Important:** this is a steady-state snapshot, not a 12-month projection. Realistic
build time from Cambridge launch to all three locations at full volume is closer to
18–20 months (see `milestones.md`).

## Insurance Checklist

- General liability
- Professional liability / errors & omissions
- Abuse/molestation liability (given minor clients)
- Non-owned auto liability (home-visit driving)
- Business Owner's Policy once the physical space exists
- Cyber liability, once the AI-listening/data component goes live
- Workers' compensation — required the moment you hire your first employee

## Massachusetts Registration Notes

- No sales tax registration needed — tutoring is a personal/professional service,
  exempt in MA
- LLC Annual Report: $500/year, every year, regardless of activity
- 1099-K federal threshold (2026): $20,000 and 200 transactions per platform — Zelle
  is exempt entirely (bank-to-bank, not a third-party settlement organization)
